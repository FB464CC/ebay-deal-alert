Conservative successful request-window upper bounds, in seconds:

  2                  24   2.763    4.683  13.684  18.318  22.823

- Only 3/128 conservative windows were at least 18s, 2/128 at least 19s,
  and 1/128 at least 20s. These are upper bounds, not HTTP durations. The
  22.823s two-listing outlier necessarily contains at least 2.823s outside
  the HTTP request because a request with a 20s Requests timeout succeeded;
  its boundary begins at the last discovery log and includes two listings'
  detail/image preparation. The 18.821s two-listing and 19.292s three-listing
  outliers likewise include preparation. By contrast, the tightest directly
  anchored singles start at the log emitted immediately before Gemini fallback
  and top out at 16.909s (next 16.765s and 16.310s), still including response
  parsing and final gating.
- There is no success pile-up at 18-20s. Even the conservative p95 is 14.976s
  for singles and 15.912s for three-listing batches. The two-listing p95 of
  18.318s is driven by preparation-inclusive first-request windows and cannot
  be treated as HTTP latency; its median is only 4.683s. The evidence therefore
  does not show calls being cut off just before success and does not support a
  larger timeout or a p95-plus-margin value.
- Request failures by submitted listing count, derived from the exact warning
  cadence and the predecessor's candidate totals: 43 timeout responses = 13

  recover it.

Checkpoint 5 - bounded-change decision
- No production/config change is supported. Raising the timeout would spend
  more of the urgent-auction run on the long tail even though successes do not
  cluster at the ceiling. An immediate 503 retry is contradicted by the
  incident cluster. A timeout retry with a smaller payload is only a hypothesis:
  pairs timed out at least as often as triples, and there are no retry outcomes
  proving recovery. Spending extra free-tier requests and run slots on that
  hypothesis is not evidence-bounded.
- Deadline audit: every normal and batched Gemini request obtains its timeout
  through `_deadline_timeout(hard_stop, GEMINI_VISION_TIMEOUT_SECONDS)`, where
  `hard_stop = run_started + RUN_BUDGET_SECONDS`. Thus even a larger configured
  timeout could not overrun the 390s internal deadline, but it could consume
  more of the remaining window and delay later auction candidates. Since the
  measured success record does not justify a higher value, the configured 20s
  remains unchanged.

Bottom line
- The 20s cutoff is not the evidenced bottleneck. Successful request-window
  upper bounds have medians of 2.58s/4.68s/6.16s and p95s of
  14.98s/18.32s/15.91s for one/two/three listings, with the high pair windows
  demonstrably containing non-HTTP preparation. Tight single-request anchors
  max at 16.91s, and there is no 18-20s success cluster.
- Timeouts are real and more frequent on batched payloads, but not uniquely on
  triples; the data does not demonstrate that a smaller immediate retry would
  recover them. HTTP 503s are overwhelmingly one two-hour provider incident,
  during which immediate following requests also returned 503.
- Per the evidence threshold, changing nothing is the bounded outcome.
