MODERN GOLF IRON / FULL-SET BUYING CRITERIA
===========================================

Purpose
-------
This is a short evidence reference for future search and rule work. It records
what to seek; it does not turn missing seller detail into a rejection. The
research basis is real web research plus the release-year findings supplied in
the 2026-09-22 owner dispatch.

What to seek
------------
- Prefer game-improvement irons: a cavity-back head with weight around the
  perimeter, a visibly wider sole, and a low/deep center of gravity. Those
  features resist twisting on off-center contact and help launch the ball. They
  are a better first-set target than compact muscle-back/blade irons.
- Use 2012 as the minimum named-model line. It is a technology boundary, not an
  arbitrary age guess: TaylorMade's 2012 RocketBallz generation is documented
  with multi-material construction and face-flex/slot technology such as the
  Speed Pocket. That design direction--a thinner, more flexible face plus
  redistributed mass--became characteristic of modern distance/forgiveness
  irons. A listing with no model name remains unknown, not automatically old.
- Specifically exclude only the evidenced pre-line models for now: TaylorMade
  Burner 2.0 (2010); Callaway X-20 (about 2004-2006), X-22 (2008), X-24
  (about 2010-2011), and Diablo Edge (January 2010); Ping G10 (2007), G15
  (2009), and G20 (2011). Do not infer a release year for an unresearched model.
- A normal useful modern iron run is commonly 4-iron or 5-iron through pitching
  wedge, sometimes with a gap/approach wedge and sand wedge. Hybrids commonly
  replace the harder-to-hit 3- and 4-irons. Therefore, a coherent 5-PW run with
  a matching hybrid can be complete for its intended job; do not reject it
  merely because there is no traditional 3- or 4-iron.

Used-club photo/text checks
---------------------------
- Faces/grooves: ask for clear face photos, especially of the short irons and
  wedges. Groove edges should remain distinct rather than polished flat; reject
  severe face wear, gouging, or rust in the hitting area.
- Shafts: sight down each shaft for straightness. Reject bent, dented, split,
  badly corroded, or heavily rusted shafts and obvious hosel/ferrule separation.
- Set consistency: confirm the club sequence and that shaft model/material and
  flex markings match across the set. A disclosed replacement can be evaluated,
  but an unexplained odd shaft/flex is not a truly matched set.
- Heads/condition: confirm every head is present and securely attached, with no
  cracks or structural rust. Worn grips are a replaceable cost; bent shafts,
  damaged heads, and worn-out grooves are the material deal breakers.
